# Purposeful community adventures

Scope: Calapan City and all 14 municipalities. Festivals and sightseeing itineraries belong in separate adventures. Existing sourced content is reused; no new historical claims were researched for this change. Legends remain labeled as accounts rather than confirmed events. Barangay totals retain the PSA record date.

Each place has five missions: history, community facts, livelihoods, traditions, and environment. Briefings show the sourced discovery and the place’s fictional sprite. Players must meet the timed objective, gather the place’s own illustrated keepsakes, and answer a topic-specific knowledge check. Wrong answers offer a review and retry; completion and first-clear rewards are withheld until the correct answer is confirmed. Existing saves and mission IDs remain compatible. Previously completed missions are not revoked.

Mission 1: collect coins and at least two keepsakes within 65 seconds, at most two hits. Mission 2: collect six to eight keepsakes and survive 40 seconds within a 70-second deadline. Mission 3: reach a score target with at most one hit and three keepsakes in 75 seconds. Mission 4: collect 22–24 coins and four keepsakes without a hit in 75 seconds. Mission 5: dodge 12–16 hazards, gather five keepsakes, and take at most one hit in 85 seconds. Locality variations are small; all 75 configurations are exercised by deterministic careful-play simulations. These demonstrate feasibility, not age suitability or human-tested difficulty.

Collectibles are fictional learning tokens, not representations of taking actual cultural objects. Each locality retains its distinct token, story sprite, and stamp. The sprite gives the briefing and required check; the token is required by the objective; the stamp and journal unlock after mission five (with earlier missions gated sequentially). Endless-play entry points have been removed. The unrelated side fox is removed from both briefings and live gameplay.

Learning games: Shape Detective (Hanunó’o sound/character matching), Word Builder (Tagalog spelling, explicitly not a Hanunó’o translation), and Community Detective (sourced facts). Starter/Growing/Challenge offer 2/3/4 choices, short/medium/long words, and 5/8/10 rounds. Age suggestions are flexible and do not restrict access; early readers can use adult help. No timer pressures the learning games. First-try round scores, attempts/correct totals, and completed game levels distinguish practice from mastery. Tracing remains manual practice, not automatic handwriting assessment.

## Content by locality

### Puerto Galera

- **History:** Puerto Galera was nominated as a UNESCO biosphere reserve in 1977, a milestone in the history of its relationship with the surrounding landscape. (Source IDs: pg_unesco)
- **Barangay count:** Puerto Galera has 13 barangays in the PSA table dated 31 July 2025. Barangays are the local communities that make up the municipality or city. (Source IDs: psa_puerto_galera)
- **Ways of living:** Fishing, subsistence farming and basket-making connect household work with the sea and land. (Source IDs: pg_unesco, pg_weaving)
- **Traditions:** Iraya-Mangyan artisans pass nito-weaving skills through families, making baskets, trays and other useful objects. This tradition belongs to its community of makers. (Source IDs: pg_weaving)
- **Environment:** Forests, mangroves and coral reefs are connected habitats. Keeping land and water healthy supports people as well as wildlife. (Source IDs: pg_unesco)

### San Teodoro

- **History:** The provincial history records Subaan as an earlier name. Act 3498 reestablished San Teodoro separately from Baco, effective 1 January 1929. (Source IDs: st_history, act3498)
- **Barangay count:** San Teodoro has 8 barangays in the PSA table dated 31 July 2025. Barangays are the local communities that make up the municipality or city. (Source IDs: psa_san_teodoro)
- **Ways of living:** Coconut processing is one documented local enterprise, turning farm produce into goods for sale. (Source IDs: enterprise)
- **Traditions:** Provincial records include Iraya communities in San Teodoro and describe nito weaving among Iraya crafts. Community makers carry their own skills and identities. (Source IDs: iraya_craft)
- **Environment:** Rivers and forested land form part of the town landscape. Water flowing downstream links upland care with community life. (Source IDs: st_history)

### Baco

- **History:** Act 3498 reestablished Baco and San Teodoro as separate municipal districts, effective 1 January 1929. (Source IDs: act3498)
- **Barangay count:** Baco has 27 barangays in the PSA table dated 31 July 2025. Barangays are the local communities that make up the municipality or city. (Source IDs: psa_baco)
- **Ways of living:** Farmer organizations in Baco participated in calamansi planting support documented in the provincial 2019 report. (Source IDs: farm_report)
- **Traditions:** One local place-name account connects Baco with paco, an edible fern. Other accounts also exist; this is an oral account, not a settled origin. (Source IDs: baco_history)
- **Environment:** Baco has rivers and upland landscapes. Riverbank planting has been used in provincial flood-mitigation efforts that include Baco. (Source IDs: baco_history, rivercare)

### Calapan City

- **History:** Calapan became a city following the plebiscite of 21 March 1998. It is Oriental Mindoro's provincial capital. (Source IDs: calapan_history)
- **Barangay count:** Calapan City has 62 barangays in the PSA table dated 31 July 2025. Barangays are the local communities that make up the municipality or city. (Source IDs: psa_calapan_city)
- **Ways of living:** Commerce and farming both form part of city life. Provincial records document support for Calapan farmer organizations growing calamansi. (Source IDs: calapan, farm_report)
- **Traditions:** Local food-making and sharing are part of Calapan's cultural life. The city government highlights local delicacies as expressions of community identity. (Source IDs: calapan_food)
- **Environment:** Coastal mangroves and fish habitats are part of Calapan's environment. Healthy coastal water supports living ecosystems. (Source IDs: calapan_mpa)

### Naujan

- **History:** A local legend connects Naujan's name with thirst. It is an origin story recorded in the town history, rather than a confirmed account of how the name began. (Source IDs: naujan_history)
- **Barangay count:** Naujan has 70 barangays in the PSA table dated 31 July 2025. Barangays are the local communities that make up the municipality or city. (Source IDs: psa_naujan)
- **Ways of living:** Freshwater fisheries support livelihoods around Naujan Lake; scientific assessments help guide sustainable management. (Source IDs: lake_fisheries)
- **Traditions:** The provincial history records religious life and places of worship in Naujan's past. Such accounts describe parts of local community life, without representing every resident's beliefs. (Source IDs: naujan_history)
- **Environment:** Naujan shares a lake ecosystem with Pola, Socorro and Victoria. Freshwater habitats connect communities across municipal boundaries. (Source IDs: lake_profile)

### Victoria

- **History:** Victoria was formed from territory previously belonging to Naujan in 1953, according to the compiled municipal history. (Source IDs: victoria_history)
- **Barangay count:** Victoria has 32 barangays in the PSA table dated 31 July 2025. Barangays are the local communities that make up the municipality or city. (Source IDs: psa_victoria)
- **Ways of living:** Rice and varied fruits support farm livelihoods. Documented crops include calamansi, bananas, lanzones, mangosteen and rambutan. (Source IDs: victoria_up)
- **Traditions:** A local account connects the earlier name Borbocolon with gushing water. Place-name stories preserve community memory, even when they are not established historical facts. (Source IDs: victoria_history)
- **Environment:** Fields, orchards and the shared lake landscape connect Victoria's food production with soil and water. (Source IDs: victoria_up, lake_profile)

### Socorro

- **History:** Republic Act 3609, approved on 22 June 1963, created Socorro from territory formerly belonging to Pola. (Source IDs: socorro_law)
- **Barangay count:** Socorro has 26 barangays in the PSA table dated 31 July 2025. Barangays are the local communities that make up the municipality or city. (Source IDs: psa_socorro)
- **Ways of living:** Calamansi, rambutan, coffee and bananas are among the crops described in the provincial municipal history. (Source IDs: ppdo_history)
- **Traditions:** The municipal account links the earlier settlement with Catiningan and explains the name Socorro through a local naming story. Treat such accounts as remembered community narratives. (Source IDs: socorro_lgu)
- **Environment:** Socorro is one of the four municipalities surrounding the shared Naujan Lake landscape. Water stewardship connects neighboring communities. (Source IDs: lake_profile)

### Pola

- **History:** Pola's historical account places the town center at a river estuary, showing the long connection between settlement and water. (Source IDs: pola_history)
- **Barangay count:** Pola has 23 barangays in the PSA table dated 31 July 2025. Barangays are the local communities that make up the municipality or city. (Source IDs: psa_pola)
- **Ways of living:** Agriculture and fishing are documented livelihoods in Pola, linking daily work to farms, rivers and coastal water. (Source IDs: pola_history)
- **Traditions:** Nipa weaving is among the local practices recorded in Pola's history. Useful woven materials connect craft knowledge with everyday needs. (Source IDs: pola_history)
- **Environment:** A river estuary connects freshwater and coastal water. Pola also shares the wider Naujan Lake landscape with neighboring municipalities. (Source IDs: pola_history, lake_profile)

### Pinamalayan

- **History:** The municipal quick-facts account dates the transfer of the town seat from its earlier location to 1916. Its planned center follows a grid layout. (Source IDs: pin_facts, pin_lgu)
- **Barangay count:** Pinamalayan has 37 barangays in the PSA table dated 31 July 2025. Barangays are the local communities that make up the municipality or city. (Source IDs: psa_pinamalayan)
- **Ways of living:** Banana-chip making is a documented local food enterprise, adding value to agricultural produce. (Source IDs: pin_facts)
- **Traditions:** A recorded origin legend tells of travelers guided toward land by a rainbow. It is a community story, not proof of the town's historical founding. (Source IDs: pin_history)
- **Environment:** Cultivated soils support farm life. Pinamalayan has also been included in riverbank planting efforts for flood mitigation. (Source IDs: soils, rivercare)

### Gloria

- **History:** Gloria's municipal history records that its territory was once part of Pinamalayan and describes settlement around Tambong in the early twentieth century. (Source IDs: gloria_history)
- **Barangay count:** Gloria has 27 barangays in the PSA table dated 31 July 2025. Barangays are the local communities that make up the municipality or city. (Source IDs: psa_gloria)
- **Ways of living:** Banana-chip processing is one documented enterprise in Gloria, connecting cultivation with food-making and trade. (Source IDs: enterprise)
- **Traditions:** Bamboo handicraft skills are shared through local training supported by DTI. Contemporary craft learning is part of living culture; it need not be described as an ancient ritual. (Source IDs: gloria_craft)
- **Environment:** Agricultural soils and riverbanks support local life. Gloria has participated in bamboo planting for flood mitigation. (Source IDs: soils, rivercare)

### Bansud

- **History:** The compiled municipal history records Bansud as formerly part of Bongabong. (Source IDs: bansud_history)
- **Barangay count:** Bansud has 13 barangays in the PSA table dated 31 July 2025. Barangays are the local communities that make up the municipality or city. (Source IDs: psa_bansud)
- **Ways of living:** Bananas and other agricultural products form part of Bansud's documented economic identity. (Source IDs: bansud_agri)
- **Traditions:** An oral tradition connects the name Bansud with basud, or delta. Keep this explanation labeled as a local account. (Source IDs: bansud_history)
- **Environment:** Riverbank vegetation helps protect waterways and nearby land. Bansud has been included in bamboo planting efforts for flood mitigation. (Source IDs: rivercare)

### Bongabong

- **History:** The compiled history preserves accounts of Bongabong's earlier settlement and name. These accounts connect the town's development with its coastal landscape. (Source IDs: bong_history)
- **Barangay count:** Bongabong has 36 barangays in the PSA table dated 31 July 2025. Barangays are the local communities that make up the municipality or city. (Source IDs: psa_bongabong)
- **Ways of living:** Coconut plantations on coastal plains are described in the provincial municipal history. Harvesting and processing link farms with household incomes. (Source IDs: bong_history)
- **Traditions:** The municipal history records a Binago-related explanation of Bongabong's name. It is one name account rather than a proven single origin. (Source IDs: bong_history)
- **Environment:** Coastal plains and river systems connect cultivation with water. Bongabong has been included in riverbank planting for flood mitigation. (Source IDs: bong_history, rivercare)

### Roxas

- **History:** Roxas's compiled history connects its development with the earlier settlement of Paclasan. (Source IDs: roxas_history)
- **Barangay count:** Roxas has 20 barangays in the PSA table dated 31 July 2025. Barangays are the local communities that make up the municipality or city. (Source IDs: psa_roxas)
- **Ways of living:** Provincial records document support for farmer organizations in Roxas, including calamansi planting materials. (Source IDs: farm_report)
- **Traditions:** A local name account connects Paclasan with the palasan palm. Place-name accounts preserve remembered relationships with plants and land. (Source IDs: roxas_history)
- **Environment:** Agricultural soils support crops such as rice, corn, coconut and root crops in the wider southern Mindoro landscape, including Roxas. (Source IDs: soils)

### Mansalay

- **History:** Act 3498 reestablished Mansalay and Bulalacao separately, effective 1 January 1929. (Source IDs: act3498)
- **Barangay count:** Mansalay has 17 barangays in the PSA table dated 31 July 2025. Barangays are the local communities that make up the municipality or city. (Source IDs: psa_mansalay)
- **Ways of living:** Provincial records document coconut farmer organizations and cacao planting support in Mansalay. (Source IDs: farm_report)
- **Traditions:** Hanunoo Mangyan ambahan uses seven-syllable lines to express experiences and relationships. NCCA associates culture bearer Ginaw Bilog with this tradition in Mansalay. (Source IDs: ambahan_ncca, ambahan_form)
- **Environment:** Farm soils and vegetation support cultivation. Caring for soil and water helps sustain agricultural livelihoods. (Source IDs: soils)

### Bulalacao

- **History:** Act 3498 reestablished Bulalacao and Mansalay separately, effective 1 January 1929. (Source IDs: act3498)
- **Barangay count:** Bulalacao has 15 barangays in the PSA table dated 31 July 2025. Barangays are the local communities that make up the municipality or city. (Source IDs: psa_bulalacao)
- **Ways of living:** A provincial enterprise report documents a Bulalacao cooperative making seaweed or kelp noodles, connecting coastal resources with food production. (Source IDs: enterprise)
- **Traditions:** The town history preserves a bird story about Bulalacao's name and explicitly identifies it as a myth. Stories can carry identity without being treated as confirmed events. (Source IDs: bulalacao_history)
- **Environment:** Mindoro's agricultural soil survey includes land in Bulalacao. Soil and water care support cultivation and community livelihoods. (Source IDs: soils)
